# Austin adaptation: research and editorial brief

Status: prototype editorial brief, not completed source verification. Official-site requests on 2026-10-24 were rejected by this cloud environment's outbound network policy. No page contents were retrieved from the official sources below. The user subsequently authorized building with placeholders instead of waiting for research access. The source register remains a reading list, not a claim that these pages were reviewed.

## Product objective

Create an English adaptation of the original Murmansk experience in this `Austin` folder. Retain its immersive canvas sky, photographic typography, scroll-driven day cycle, circular year calendar, photo ribbons, city carousel, route storytelling, trip builder, practical information, and shareable itinerary. Adapt the subject matter and calculations to Austin rather than replacing city names in the Russian text.

The candidate narrative is **Austin, from first light to last song**: how the same place changes between a waterside morning, a hot afternoon, sunset at the bridge, and an evening of live music. Research must establish whether each part earns its place. The Arctic's polar night and aurora narrative cannot be carried over as an Austin experience.

## Research standard

- Prefer the operator, responsible public agency, or official destination organization for each fact.
- Save the source URL, retrieved date, exact supporting passage, proposed site wording, and any uncertainty for every material claim.
- Cross-check seasonal wildlife, swimming rules, weather, and reservation requirements against a second authoritative source where practical.
- Separate astronomical calculations, climatological patterns, current operating conditions, and editorial recommendations.
- Do not turn average weather into a forecast or seasonal wildlife into a guaranteed encounter.
- Avoid hard-coded prices, opening hours, transit headways, festival dates, and booking availability without a refresh plan.
- Give uncertain or changing claims an official check-before-you-go link.
- Record why a candidate belongs or was rejected, not only a list of places.

## Candidate content: include only after verification

### 1. A day in Austin

Use a sky that evolves with scrolling, Austin's actual latitude and longitude, the `America/Chicago` time zone, daylight-saving transitions, and sunrise/sunset estimates. Use an Austin skyline and low Hill Country silhouettes rather than Arctic mountains. Fill the large AUSTIN letterforms with actual local photographs.

The interface must distinguish a scroll-controlled simulated clock from the actual current local time. Date selection changes the sun and itinerary context. It must not claim that a selected date is today's live weather.

### 2. A year around the sun

Keep the 365/366-day interactive circular calendar. Let keyboard, pointer, and touch select a date, show approximate daylight, and apply it to the trip builder. Explain that summer daylight length and outdoor comfort are different things. Do not invent a numerical comfort score or monthly rain probability.

Potential seasonal editorial layers: spring outdoor mornings, summer shade and water, autumn outdoor time, and cooler winter city days. Verify their limits before presenting them as travel advice. Show solstices/equinoxes as approximate seasonal markers unless using a precise astronomical source.

### 3. City carousel

Candidates to evaluate for variety rather than popularity alone:

- Lady Bird Lake and the Butler Trail: geographic anchor, sunrise and skyline photography; verify swimming rules separately from permitted boating.
- Barton Springs Pool: an Austin-specific water experience; link to the operator for access and closures.
- Congress Avenue Bridge: an evening wildlife story, with no guaranteed bat emergence time or sighting.
- Texas State Capitol: architecture and civic history, plus an indoor alternative.
- South Congress: street life and independent shops; avoid equating one shopping district with the whole city.
- East Austin: research specific cultural places and history rather than presenting the area only as bars and restaurants.
- A live-music district or venue context: distinguish live music from generic nightlife; check age restrictions and accessibility before recommending a specific show.

Do not use unrelated Texas desert, mountain, or ranch stock photos as if they depict Austin. Avoid presenting alcohol as a required part of an itinerary.

### 4. Three narrative routes

Candidate route A, **Lake to city lights**: a city-based walk linking waterfront, civic/streetscape stops, and evening activity. Check realistic walking distances, shade, road crossings, and alternatives for limited mobility.

Candidate route B, **Springs and shaded city**: a pool visit and nearby cultural/food options. Include closures and a non-swimming fallback. Do not direct visitors to swim in a lake or creek on the basis of a generic summer recommendation.

Candidate route C, **Into the Hill Country**: select one realistic day trip after researching park access, drive time, heat, and reservations. Pedernales Falls, McKinney Falls, and Enchanted Rock are candidates, not interchangeable stops on one day. Hamilton Pool is a candidate only if its access constraints can be explained clearly.

Each route needs researched photographs, approximate travel context, an access-check link, and a weather fallback. Sunset photography cannot override park closing times. Return driving should not be described as safe merely because the sun has set.

### 5. After dark

Replace the aurora section with an Austin-relevant night story: city lights, music, and bats in their actual season. If stargazing appears, explain the distinction between the urban sky and a suitable destination outside the city, and verify access after dark. The city itself must not be advertised as a dark-sky reserve.

Do not map hours of darkness to bat probability or music availability. No fabricated forecast charts. Any chart must label what it actually measures.

### 6. Trip builder

Potential goals: waterfront walks, swimming, food, live music, cultural stops, bats, photography, and a Hill Country day. Support arrival date, trip length, transportation preference, and a relaxed/active pace if needed to prevent unrealistic plans.

Rules that must be researched and tested:

- Wildlife is seasonal and uncertain; display alternatives when bats are outside the usual season.
- Summer outdoor recommendations favor morning/evening and shade; do not infer safety from month alone.
- Creek flow and swimming access are not guaranteed.
- Day trips require realistic transport and reservation caveats.
- The plan cannot imply it has booked a pool, park, restaurant, or performance.
- A downtown day cannot silently require a car after the visitor chose walking/transit.
- Departures and arrivals leave realistic time rather than allocating a full excursion day.
- Date changes refresh every dependent recommendation.
- Sharing/copying preserves date, duration, and chosen goals, with a readable fallback if clipboard access fails.

### 7. Practical visitor information

Research airport transport, downtown movement, parking, hydration and heat, thunderstorm/flash-flood advisories, park reservations, accessibility links, and appropriate clothing. Official advisories remain the authority for current conditions. Keep safety information attached to the activity where it changes the travel decision.

Provide a water-based alternative, indoor alternative, and lower-mobility alternative only when research supports them; do not claim universal accessibility for an entire route.

## Image and graphic requirements

- Download actual Austin/Hill Country photographs from sources with recorded reuse terms.
- Keep an `ASSET_CREDITS.md` ledger: local filename, subject, photographer, original file page, license, required attribution, and any crop/edit.
- Include responsive image sizes, meaningful alternative text, and lazy loading outside the first screen.
- Use the original site's navy, warm paper, amber, rounded cards, and photographic display type as a starting point; choose Austin-relevant accent colors after reviewing the images.
- Replace the lichen/Arctic motif with a restrained local botanical or water-line motif. Do not label generated illustrations as photographs.
- Keep original design attribution distinguishable from Austin editorial/photographic credits.
- Package fonts and animation libraries locally where their licenses permit it, so the Austin site does not depend on the blocked third-party hosts used by the original.

## Original implementation audit

The original repository is available under `murmansk-studio`. It combines CSS, HTML, and most JavaScript in a single 1,863-line HTML file, with local WebP images. Its GSAP/ScrollTrigger code supplies the pinned day cycle, word reveals, photo ribbons, and route progress. Google Fonts supplies Dela Gothic One, Inter, and Tenor Sans.

Reusable mechanics: photographic canvas word mask, layered sky, ring/strip date selection, route progress, card carousel, itinerary ticket, mobile navigation, and reduced-motion support.

Required rewrites: Arctic astronomy constants and fixed UTC+3 clock; aurora rendering; polar-season classifications; all trip-fit and itinerary rules; event names; packing advice; geographic imagery; Russian pluralization/date labels; author/social/referral links; daylight labels; calendar leap-year handling. A literal translation would preserve incorrect assumptions for Austin.

## Acceptance checks after research and implementation

1. Every visitor-facing string is English; no Arctic place names or travel rules remain.
2. All geographical photographs have verified subjects and recorded rights/credits.
3. All material travel claims have a source and a confidence/refresh decision.
4. Solstice daylight is plausible for Austin and materially different from the Arctic; leap years have 366 selectable days.
5. Clock and sunrise/sunset labels remain correct across US daylight-saving changes and a browser in another time zone.
6. Ring, year strip, date chips, and itinerary all agree on the selected date.
7. Planner combinations do not produce contradictory travel modes or unsupported seasonal promises.
8. Keyboard, touch, mobile navigation, reduced motion, contrast, focus, and overflow checks pass.
9. Browser reports no missing local assets or runtime errors; the page works without third-party font/animation hosts.
10. The Austin folder can be served independently and under a repository subpath. Public deployment is treated separately from building the folder.

## Prototype decision and verification boundary

The user authorized a working prototype with placeholder imagery and provisional editorial content. Implementation proceeded under that instruction. All scene images are locally generated, stylized SVG illustrations and are labeled as illustrated previews. No stock photography is misrepresented as a verified Austin photograph. No invented weather, opening times, prices, event listings, or live availability are shown.

The built narrative is “Austin, from first light to last song.” Water, civic culture, street life, food, music, and a conditional Hill Country day are editorial starting points. Bat viewing remains conditional and seasonal; pool and park access require checking the operator. Official links help visitors check current information, but their live contents have not been verified here.

Astronomical values are calculated from Austin coordinates using an approximate solar model. Time-zone offsets use the IANA America/Chicago zone. These are tested calculations, not values copied from an unvisited source. They are explicitly approximate and are not weather forecasts.
